# What You Can Build After This Course

The course teaches you the foundation. This catalog shows where it goes. Everything below is a pattern the team at [Till CFO](https://tillcfo.com) actually runs internally — built mostly by non-engineers, using exactly the approach you learn in Module 5: describe the workflow, let Claude build it, refine it as you use it.

These are descriptions, not downloads. Each one is buildable in an afternoon once you've finished the course — and the bundled `/gl-review` skill is a finished example of the pattern, so you can see what "done" looks like.

**Difficulty key:** 🟢 buildable straight after Module 5 · 🟡 takes a connected tool (calendar, email, Slack) · 🔴 multi-source, build it once you've done a few 🟡s

---

## Daily Rhythm

**Morning brief** 🟡
Every weekday morning: today's calendar, tasks due, approaching deadlines, and anything in the inbox that needs a decision — assembled into one scannable message before you've had coffee. The single highest-leverage automation for most operators.

**Afternoon recap** 🟡
The end-of-day mirror: what got done, what's still open, what came out of today's meetings, and what tomorrow-you needs to know. Catches the follow-ups that die between 4pm and the next morning.

**Deadline watchdog** 🟢
A skill that scans your task list and projects for anything overdue, due today, or quietly approaching — and ranks the output by urgency instead of by list order. Works on a plain markdown task file before you ever connect a tool.

**Calendar density review** 🟡
Once a week, look at the week ahead: back-to-back blocks, days with no focus time, meetings that could be async, and missing prep time before the ones that matter.

## Communications

**Inbox triage** 🟡
Categorize the inbox by urgency, flag what's awaiting your reply versus theirs, and draft response options for the urgent ones — in your voice, because your CLAUDE.md already knows it.

**Meeting prep brief** 🟡
Before an important meeting: who's attending, what happened last time, what's still open, and what you want out of the room — one page, assembled from your notes and email automatically.

**Meeting follow-up extractor** 🟢
Paste a transcript or raw notes, get back decisions, action items with owners, and a drafted follow-up message. The Module 3 exercise, turned into a one-command habit.

**SOP generator** 🟢
Describe a process out loud (or paste the Slack thread where someone explained it), get a clean, followable SOP in your team's standard format. Compounds fast in growing teams.

## Finance

**GL first-pass review** 🟢 — *included with this course as `/gl-review`*
Point it at any general ledger export and get ranked findings — duplicates, miscodings, recognition flags, unexplained spikes — plus client-ready questions. The Module 6 exercise, productized.

**Month-end close checklist runner** 🟡
Your close checklist as a living skill: it walks the list, checks what it can verify from exports you give it, and produces a status-with-exceptions summary instead of a wall of checkboxes.

**Invoice / payroll-report reconciliation** 🔴
Compare two documents that should agree — a payroll provider invoice against the register, a vendor statement against the ledger — and surface every line that doesn't tie, with a plain-English explanation of each gap. One of the highest-ROI patterns on this list.

**Board-deck first drafts** 🟡
Feed it the month's financials and your notes; get the narrative draft — performance vs. plan, what changed, what to watch — in your reporting voice. You edit judgment, not blank pages.

## Team & Ops

**Delegation tracker** 🟡
A weekly sweep of everything you've handed off: what's moving, what's stale, what needs a nudge — so follow-up stops living in your head.

**Onboarding comms pack** 🟢
Give it a new hire's name, role, and start date; get the welcome email, team announcement, and first-week check-in drafts in one pass, warm and consistent every time.

**Project health check** 🟡
A Monday scan of active projects for staleness, deadline risk, and momentum signals — with a short "needs attention" list instead of a status meeting.

## Going Further

**Your team's decision playbook** 🔴
Capture how your senior-most people make calls — the principles, the precedents, the questions they ask first — into a living document Claude can consult when they're not in the room. The most ambitious pattern here, and the one teams end up valuing most.

**A skill marketplace for your team** 🔴
Once a few people are building skills, put them in a shared git repo your whole team installs from — exactly how this course reached you. One person's afternoon project becomes everyone's tool.
