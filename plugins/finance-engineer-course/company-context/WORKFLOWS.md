# Meridian — Common Operational Workflows

These are 8 workflows that happen regularly at Meridian. They represent the kinds of recurring work where Claude can reduce friction, cut time, and improve output quality. Each workflow is documented from a real operational perspective — not idealized, but as it actually runs.

---

## 1. Weekly Team Standup Prep

**Frequency:** Weekly (typically Monday morning)
**Primary Owner:** Operations Coordinator
**Supporting:** Chief of Staff / CoS Support

**What It Involves**
Before the weekly all-hands or team standup, someone needs to pull together the current state of projects, surface any blockers, and prepare a brief that leadership can actually use. That means checking the project tracker in Notion, scanning Slack for anything that surfaced over the weekend or late last week, reviewing open tasks, and synthesizing it all into a readable update.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Pulling from multiple sources (Notion project tracker, Slack threads, individual task lists) and reconciling them into a coherent picture. The data exists — it's just scattered. Writing the standup doc from scratch each week even when 70% of it is a variation on last week's.

**How Claude Can Help**
Given a dump of project statuses and Slack highlights, Claude can synthesize a clean standup brief in a consistent format. It can also identify which projects are on track, which have gone quiet (potential stall risk), and which have open action items that haven't moved. The Ops Coordinator reviews and edits — Claude eliminates the blank-page problem and the copy-paste assembly work.

## 2. Client Meeting Pre-Brief

**Frequency:** Ad hoc (before any significant client call)
**Primary Owner:** EA / CoS Support
**Supporting:** Consulting team member running the meeting

**What It Involves**
Before a client call — especially a QBR, a fundraising strategy session, or a new engagement kickoff — someone needs to prepare a brief. This typically includes: who's attending from the client side, what was discussed in the last meeting, what open items exist, what the goal of this meeting is, and any relevant context from recent emails or Slack.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Pulling context from multiple sources: Fireflies transcripts from the last call, relevant emails, Notion notes from previous meetings, and sometimes Slack messages from the client's channel. Stitching these together into a one-page brief by hand takes 30–60 minutes, and it often feels rushed because the brief is needed right before the call.

**How Claude Can Help**
With access to a Fireflies transcript from the last call, a few recent emails, and a description of the meeting's purpose, Claude can produce a structured one-page brief: attendees, last-meeting summary, open items, suggested agenda, and key questions to address. The consultant or CoS reviews and trims — the 60-minute task becomes 10 minutes.

## 3. Project Status Update to Leadership

**Frequency:** Weekly
**Primary Owner:** Operations Coordinator / CoS
**Supporting:** Project owners across the team

**What It Involves**
Each week, a status update goes to Drew and the leadership team covering the state of all active internal projects and rocks (quarterly priorities). This is distinct from the team standup — it's a leadership-facing artifact. It needs to be crisp, accurate, and highlight anything that needs a decision or attention.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Chasing down status from project owners, reconciling what's in Notion versus what people have told you verbally or in Slack, and then writing the update in a way that's honest about risk without creating unnecessary alarm. The drafting itself is straightforward but time-consuming because it involves synthesizing a lot of inputs.

**How Claude Can Help**
Given structured project data (status, owner, last update, blockers) from Notion, Claude can draft a clean leadership status update in a consistent format. It can flag items that look stalled (no update in X days), surface blockers that need leadership input, and keep the language concise. The Ops Coordinator adds judgment and context — Claude handles the assembly and formatting.

## 4. New Hire Onboarding Communications

**Frequency:** Ad hoc (every new hire)
**Primary Owner:** People and Culture Coordinator
**Supporting:** Hiring manager, EA / CoS Support

**What It Involves**
Every time a new person joins Meridian, a set of communications needs to go out: a welcome email to the new hire (before day one), a team announcement in Slack, a first-week check-in message, and often a brief sent to the hiring manager outlining what the new hire needs to do in their first week. Each of these needs to feel personal and warm while also being accurate and complete.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Writing each communication from scratch or adapting a previous version that may have outdated details. Calibrating the tone — professional but warm, specific enough to feel personal without being over the top. Making sure every communication has the right practical information (start date, point of contact, tools access, first-week schedule).

**How Claude Can Help**
Given a standard input form with the new hire's name, role, start date, manager, and a few details about their background, Claude can produce the full communication set: welcome email, Slack announcement, first-week check-in, and manager brief. The People Coordinator personalizes and reviews — Claude ensures nothing is missing and the tone is consistent.

## 5. Inbox Triage and Response Drafting

**Frequency:** Daily
**Primary Owner:** Everyone (especially EA/CoS Support, Ops Coordinator)
**Supporting:** N/A

**What It Involves**
Email volume at Meridian is high. On any given day, there are client questions, vendor communications, internal coordination emails, meeting requests, and a long tail of lower-priority messages that still need a response. Someone has to read it all, decide what's urgent, and draft or send responses. For the EA/CoS role, this includes managing the executive inbox and drafting replies on Drew's behalf.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Reading every message to determine urgency, then drafting thoughtful responses to the ones that need them. Responses that require looking up information (checking a calendar, pulling a status from Notion, referencing a previous email thread) take disproportionately long. Drafting responses from scratch for emails that have a predictable structure (scheduling requests, status questions, follow-ups).

**How Claude Can Help**
Given a set of emails, Claude can triage them by urgency, summarize what each one needs, and draft response options for the highest-priority ones. For scheduling emails, it can draft a reply with suggested times. For client questions, it can draft a response that the sender reviews and fills in with specific details. The human reviews and sends — Claude eliminates the decision fatigue of starting from zero on every message.

## 6. Post-Meeting Notes and Action Item Capture

**Frequency:** Ad hoc (after any significant meeting)
**Primary Owner:** Everyone
**Supporting:** EA / CoS Support for executive meetings

**What It Involves**
After a meeting ends, someone should capture what was discussed, what decisions were made, and what action items came out of it — with owners and due dates. At Meridian, Fireflies handles transcription automatically. But a transcript is not meeting notes. Someone still needs to turn a 45-minute conversation into a 1-page summary with a clean action item list.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Reading or listening back through the transcript to find the relevant moments. Distinguishing between things that were discussed (but not decided) versus decisions that were made versus action items that were assigned. Writing the summary in a way that's accurate and useful for people who weren't in the room.

**How Claude Can Help**
Given a Fireflies transcript, Claude can produce a structured summary: decisions made, discussion items (not decided), and a numbered action item list with owner and due date (if mentioned). This takes a 30-minute task to under 5 minutes. The meeting owner reviews, adds any context that was implicit in the room, and posts or distributes.

## 7. SOP Documentation from Verbal Process

**Frequency:** Ad hoc
**Primary Owner:** Operations Coordinator
**Supporting:** Subject matter expert (whoever owns the process)

**What It Involves**
When a process exists in someone's head — or has been described verbally in a meeting or Slack thread — someone needs to turn it into a written SOP that another person could actually follow. This happens often at Meridian because the team is growing and processes that used to live with one person need to be formalized so they can be handed off or replicated.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
The gap between "I understand how this works" and "I have written it clearly enough for a new person to follow it without asking me questions." The Ops Coordinator often knows the process but the writing takes time — structuring the steps, deciding the right level of detail, making sure edge cases and exceptions are covered.

**How Claude Can Help**
Given a verbal description, a rough bullet list of steps, or a Slack thread where a process was explained, Claude can produce a clean first-draft SOP in a standard format: purpose, scope, prerequisites, step-by-step instructions, exceptions. The Ops Coordinator reviews for accuracy and fills in any gaps — Claude handles the structure and the prose, reducing the drafting time by 60–80%.

## 8. Internal Announcement Drafting

**Frequency:** Ad hoc
**Primary Owner:** People and Culture Coordinator / Marketing Coordinator
**Supporting:** Leadership (approves before sending)

**What It Involves**
Internal announcements cover a wide range of situations: a new hire joining, a policy change, a leadership update, an upcoming team event, a change to a tool or process. Each one needs to be clear, appropriately toned, and complete — it should answer the questions people will have before they have to ask them.

**What Currently Takes the Most Time**
Calibrating tone for the situation. A policy change that might read as restrictive needs to be framed carefully. A new hire announcement should feel warm without being over the top. A process change needs to be clear about what's changing and why. Writing something that gets the tone right on the first draft is hard, and multiple revision cycles slow things down.

**How Claude Can Help**
Given a brief description of what's being announced, who the audience is, and any sensitivities to keep in mind, Claude can produce a first-draft announcement in Slack and email format. It will default to clear, warm, and professional — and can be directed toward a specific tone if the situation calls for it. The drafter reviews for accuracy and tone fit, then routes for approval. The blank-page problem is eliminated.
